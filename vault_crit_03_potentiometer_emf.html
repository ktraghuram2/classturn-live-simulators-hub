01 / Aim

To compare the electromotive forces (EMF) of two primary cells and determine the internal resistance of a primary cell using a potentiometer.

02 / Apparatus

  • Four-Wire Potentiometer Board (400 cm uniform constantan wire with brass strip interconnects and 100 cm scale) — Provides uniform potential gradient along wire
  • Precision Sliding Jockey (Spring-loaded knife-edge contact with insulated handle) — Locates null balance position along wire
  • Centre-Zero Sensitive Galvanometer (±30 divisions, least count 1 div, high sensitivity (10 μA/div)) — Detects null condition where no current flows
  • Driver Battery & Rheostat (Regulated DC power supply 0–4 V, 50 Ω rheostat) — Supplies stable primary current through wire
  • Primary Cells & Two-Way Key (Leclanché cell (1.45 V), Daniell cell (1.08 V), resistance box 0–100 Ω) — Test cells whose EMF and internal resistance are evaluated

03 / Principles

A potentiometer operates on the principle that when a steady current flows through a wire of uniform cross-section and composition, the potential drop across any length is directly proportional to that length: V=k·l, where k is the potential gradient. At the null balance point, no current is drawn from the cell, allowing measurement of the true open-circuit electromotive force without terminal loading error.

  • Principle of Potentiometer:
    Potential difference along a uniform wire segment is directly proportional to balancing length.
    V=k·l
    Where:
    • V : Potential difference across wire segment (V)
    • k : Potential gradient along the wire (Vcm)
    • l : Balancing length from zero reference end (cm)

04 / Procedure

Set up the driver circuit to pass a steady current through the 400 cm potentiometer wire. Connect the primary cells through the two-way key to the galvanometer and sliding jockey. Locate the balancing length where the galvanometer needle rests exactly at zero.

  1. Step 1: Connect the positive terminal of the driver battery to zero-end A of the potentiometer wire.
  2. Step 2: Connect the positive terminals of cells E1 and E2 to point A, and negative terminals through the two-way key to the galvanometer.
  3. Step 3: Press the jockey near end A and then near end B to verify opposite galvanometer deflections.
  4. Step 4: Slide the jockey gently along the wires to find the exact null point l1 where galvanometer deflection is zero.
  5. Step 5: Switch to cell E2 and locate balancing length l2; compute ratio E1E2.
  6. Step 6: Introduce shunt resistance R across cell E1; measure new balancing length l2' and determine internal resistance r.

07 / Validation and limits

  • Assumption: The potentiometer wire has uniform cross-sectional area and specific resistance along its entire 400 cm length.
  • Assumption: The driver battery EMF is strictly greater than the EMF of both primary test cells (E0>E1, E2).
  • Assumption: Driver current remains constant and heating effects are negligible during observation.
  • Boundary Limit: Jockey must not be scraped along the wire to prevent altering wire cross-section and destroying uniform potential gradient.
  • Boundary Limit: Balancing length measurement accuracy is bounded by least count of millimeter scale (±0.1 cm).
