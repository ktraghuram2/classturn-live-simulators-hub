01 / Aim

To resolve, separate, and identify the principal protein constituents of avian egg white (ovalbumin, ovotransferrin, ovomucoid, and lysozyme) using two-dimensional gel electrophoresis, coupling first-dimension isoelectric focusing across an immobilized pH gradient with second-dimension SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis based on molecular weight.

02 / Apparatus

  • IPG Strip & Isoelectric Focusing Chamber (Immobilized pH Gradient strip (pH 3–10 non-linear, 11 cm length, 3 mm width, polyacrylamide matrix covalently bonded to GelBond plastic backing)) — Separates denatured egg white proteins along a continuous immobilized pH gradient in the first dimension according to their characteristic isoelectric points (pI)
  • Focusing Tray & High-Voltage Electrode Assembly (Precision ceramic focusing tray with platinum wire electrodes, silicone oil overlay reservoir, and 0–3500 V programmable DC power supply) — Houses the rehydrated IPG strips under mineral oil to prevent evaporation, establishing high electric field strength for steady-state isoelectric focusing
  • Slow Rocker & Equilibration Reservoir (Variable-speed laboratory platform rocker (5–30 rpm, tilt angle ±5°) with dedicated 15 mL multi-well equilibration incubation troughs) — Gently agitates the focused IPG strips during sequential equilibration in DTT reduction buffer and iodoacetamide alkylation buffer containing 2% SDS
  • Vertical SDS-PAGE Gel Cassette & Tank (1.0 mm vertical dual-plate glass cassette holding 12% Tris-Glycine resolving polyacrylamide slab gel (10 cm × 10 cm), agarose sealing overlay, and running buffer tank) — Executes second-dimension orthogonal electrophoresis, migrating SDS-coated negatively charged proteins through the crosslinked molecular sieve proportional to log(Mr)
  • Gel Doc Imaging Chamber & Transilluminator (High-resolution CCD darkroom imaging cabinet with dual UV (302 nm) and blue-LED transilluminator, Coomassie/silver-stain optical filters, and analysis software) — Captures optical density maps of Coomassie Brilliant Blue stained protein spots on the 2D slab gel, determining spot coordinates (pI, Mr) and relative abundance

03 / Principles

Two-dimensional gel electrophoresis (2D-PAGE) is a high-resolution proteomic separation technique that resolves complex protein mixtures across two independent, orthogonal physicochemical dimensions. Avian egg white represents an ideal multi-component biological system consisting of distinct globular proteins with well-characterized isoelectric points and molecular masses: ovalbumin (~45 kDa, pI 4.5), ovotransferrin/conalbumin (~76 kDa, pI 6.0), ovomucoid (~28 kDa, pI 4.1), and lysozyme (~14.3 kDa, pI 9.3). In the first dimension, isoelectric focusing (IEF) separates proteins purely by charge across an immobilized pH gradient (IPG). An electric field drives amphoteric protein molecules until each reaches the unique pH position where its net surface charge is exactly zero (the isoelectric point, pI). Once focused, the strip is equilibrated in sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS), dithiothreitol (DTT) to reduce disulfide bridges, and iodoacetamide to alkylate free cysteines. The equilibrated strip is mounted atop a vertical polyacrylamide slab gel for second-dimension SDS-PAGE, where SDS imparts a uniform negative charge-to-mass ratio and proteins migrate through the porous polyacrylamide meshwork solely as an inverse function of molecular weight according to the Ferguson relation.

  • Isoelectric Focusing Net Charge Equilibrium:
    At the isoelectric point, the net electrical charge of the protein molecule vanishes, bringing electrophoretic migration to a complete steady-state halt

04 / Procedure

The experiment is conducted systematically in four consecutive operational phases: rehydration of IPG strips with prepared egg white sample, first-dimension isoelectric focusing, two-step chemical reduction and alkylation on the rocker platform, and second-dimension SDS-PAGE separation followed by automated Gel Doc optical density imaging.

  1. Step 1: Dilute clarified fresh egg white extract into denaturing rehydration buffer (8 M urea, 2% CHAPS, 20 mM DTT, 0.5% carrier ampholytes, trace bromophenol blue).
  2. Step 2: Pipette 200 µL of sample into the ceramic focusing tray channel, position the 11 cm IPG strip (gel-side down) avoiding air bubbles, and overlay with 1 mL high-viscosity mineral oil.
  3. Step 3: Execute first-dimension isoelectric focusing by ramping the power supply up to 2000 V (accumulating ~10,000 to 14,000 V·h) until current drops to a stable baseline (<15 µA).
  4. Step 4: Transfer focused IPG strip to the equilibration reservoir on the slow rocker; incubate for 15 min in Equilibration Buffer I (DTT reduction), followed by 15 min in Equilibration Buffer II (iodoacetamide alkylation).
  5. Step 5: Rinse the equilibrated strip in 1× Tris-Glycine-SDS running buffer and carefully mount horizontally onto the upper surface of a 12% polyacrylamide resolving gel cassette, sealing with 0.5% molten agarose containing bromophenol blue.
  6. Step 6: Assemble the gel cassette into the vertical electrophoresis chamber, fill upper (cathode) and lower (anode) tanks with running buffer, and run at 30 mA constant current until the blue dye front reaches the bottom margin.
  7. Step 7: Disassemble cassette, fix gel in 40% methanol / 10% acetic acid, stain with Coomassie Brilliant Blue R-250, destain, and acquire 2D proteomic spot map on the Gel Doc transilluminator.

07 / Validation and limits

  • Assumption: Proteins are completely denatured into single linear polypeptide chains with all intra- and inter-molecular disulfide bonds fully reduced by DTT and alkylated by iodoacetamide.
  • Assumption: SDS binds stoichiometrically at a constant mass ratio of 1.4 g SDS per 1.0 g protein, conferring a uniform negative charge-to-mass ratio and identical charge density across all species.
  • Assumption: The immobilized pH gradient (pH 3–10) in the IPG strip remains strictly linear and chemically stable under high electric field without cathodic drift.
  • Assumption: Pore size distribution throughout the crosslinked polyacrylamide matrix is homogeneous, with sieving resistance obeying the classical Ferguson log-linear relationship.
  • Boundary Limit: Extremely basic proteins with isoelectric points exceeding pH 10.5 (such as certain lysozyme isoforms) may migrate off the cathodic end of standard pH 3–10 strips.
  • Boundary Limit: Very high molecular weight glycoproteins (>150 kDa, like ovomacroglobulin/ovomucin complexes) show restricted entry into standard 12% acrylamide resolving gels and require gradient gels.
  • Boundary Limit: Excessive Joule heating resulting from uncooled high-voltage runs (>3500 V) or excessive buffer conductivity causes band broadening, vertical smiling, and protein denaturation artifacts.
